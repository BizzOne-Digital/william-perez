### Pillar 1 — H: Honest Protection for Seniors, Families & Neighborhoods
**Framing copy:** Fighting the fraud epidemic hitting our seniors hardest. California lost $1.4 billion to elder fraud in 2025 alone — up 68% from the year before, and the worst total of any state in the country (FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report). Our seniors and families deserve a coordinated local response, not a pamphlet.

Initiatives (use as sub-cards/bullets):
1. **Senior Fraud Prevention Task Force** — Stand up a council-resolution task force coordinating the city, police, banks, and senior centers on fraud prevention.
2. **Bank-Branch Scam Training** — Bring Senior Scam Stopper seminars and AARP BankSafe teller training to Inglewood bank branches, so tellers are equipped to spot exploitation before money leaves an account.
3. **One Clear Hotline** — Standardize promotion of the LA County Elder Abuse Hotline across every city touchpoint, so residents always know where to turn.
4. **Connected Senior Services** — Use the senior center network to connect seniors with transportation, home-repair, and benefits referrals they already qualify for.

**Callout stat:** $200M — AARP's BankSafe-trained tellers have helped stop an estimated $200 million in attempted financial exploitation nationally since the program's rollout, with trained staff intervening roughly 12 times more often than untrained staff (average recovered per intervention: $865 vs. $70). *Source: AARP / Virginia Tech pilot study*

### Pillar 2 — A: Action on Affordability
**Framing copy:** Closing loopholes, cutting hidden costs, restoring assistance. Roughly two-thirds of District 1 households rent, and home prices here have risen sharply while incomes have not kept pace. The average District 1 household earns about $46,400 a year, compared to roughly $61,000 countywide — and every dollar of hidden cost matters.

Initiatives:
1. **Close the Rent-Cap Loophole** — Inglewood currently allows annual rent increases of 3.7% for buildings with 5+ units, but 8.7% for buildings with 4 or fewer — a gap that leaves tenants in smaller buildings exposed. William will push to close it. *(Source: City of Inglewood, FY2025-26)*
2. **Cut the Hidden Utility Tax** — Inglewood's Utility Users Tax adds roughly 10% to electric, gas, and water bills. He'll fight to reduce this hidden charge on every household's basic needs.
3. **Restore Rental Assistance** — Advocate for direct rental assistance and reopening/prioritizing the closed Section 8 / Housing Choice Voucher waitlist for Inglewood households — an estimated 84 households are currently at risk of losing existing subsidy.
4. **Local-Hire Requirements That Deliver** — Enforce the city's 35% local-hire requirement on major development projects, so Inglewood residents see real jobs from growth happening in their own backyard.

**Callout stat/budget block:** $500K — Proposed affordability pilot: a Utility Gap Fund ($175K), a Housing Stability Fund ($125K), tenant & legal navigation services ($85K), outreach and enrollment ($45K), and enforcement/data tracking ($45K), with the remainder for administration and evaluation — every dollar itemized and reported quarterly.

### Pillar 3 — T: Transparency & Accountable City Hall
**Framing copy:** Open books, plain language, real oversight. Residents shouldn't need a law degree to understand how the city spends their money. Accountability starts with information residents can actually use.

Initiatives:
1. **Publish Signage Revenue** — Make an itemized public accounting of billboard and digital signage revenue and spending, so residents can see exactly where that money goes.
2. **Plain-Language Budget Reports** — Launch a quarterly budget report written for residents, not accountants — clear categories, clear numbers, clear comparisons.
3. **A Town Hall in Every Quadrant** — Hold quarterly town halls across all four District 1 quadrants, on a predictable schedule, so no neighborhood is an afterthought.
4. **Resident Oversight on Major Contracts** — Push for structured resident input and oversight on the city's largest development and vendor contracts before they're finalized.
